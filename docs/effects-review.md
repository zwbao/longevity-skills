# Effects table: person review checklist

This is the worklist for the human review that PLAN WS-G.2 asks for. Every row of `data/effects.jsonl` was matched by a script against the verbatim quote in the open full text (`verified_by: quote_match`). A script cannot tell whether the quote is the right number for the row. A person checks that, then edits the row in `data/effects.jsonl`:

```json
"verified": true, "verified_by": "person", "verified_by_name": "<your name>", "verified_on": "YYYY-MM-DD"
```

Automated agents never write `verified_by: "person"`. `python3 -m tools.lsk check` prints the share of rows checked by a person and fails nothing on it; it does fail a person row without name and date.

## What to check on every row

1. Open the DOI. The full text must be open (no full text, no row).
2. Find the quote at the locator. It must be in the paper word for word, and it must be the result for this intervention and this marker (not a subgroup, a sensitivity analysis or a different comparison, unless `note_zh` says so).
3. Value, both CI ends, unit and sign match the paper. The sign means intervention minus control: a fall is negative.
4. `effect.kind` is right (see the column). An SMD written as a mean difference is the most harmful mistake, because the predictor would read it in lab units.
5. `population`, `design`, `trials`, `participants` and `duration_weeks` match the paper (for a meta-analysis: the number of trials and people in this pooled estimate, when the paper gives it).
6. `intervention_zh`, `keywords` and `note_zh` describe the intervention fairly in plain Chinese, and `note_zh` names any important limit (high heterogeneity, special population, short duration).
7. If `tau_pct` is set: the tau quote reports between-person variability of the response, and the conversion in `tau_source.note` is right.

Verdict: `ok` (set person fields), `fix` (write what to change), or `drop` (the number is not usable).
